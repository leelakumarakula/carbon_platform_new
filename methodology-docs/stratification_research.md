# Stratification: whole farms, or strata inside farms?

Research note, 2026-10-05. Question: in carbon methodologies, must a stratum consist of whole farms or fields, or can (or should) one farm or field be split into several strata? What is the unit of stratification, and what rules apply to strata boundaries, minimum size, sampling points per stratum, and changes over time?

Sources: the local text copies in `methodology-docs/_text/` (page numbers are the printed page numbers of the PDFs), plus three documents downloaded for this note: CAR Soil Enrichment Protocol v1.1, FAO GSOC-MRV Protocol (2020) and the de Gruijter et al. (2016) abstract. Quotes are verbatim. "[...]" marks an omission. Where a source was only seen through a search snippet, the note says so.

---

## 0. Bottom line

* **No methodology requires a stratum to be a whole farm.** Farms are an ownership and enrolment unit, not a stratification unit. Most SOC methodologies define strata by **homogeneity of the target variable** (soil, climate, topography, management), and that homogeneity often changes inside a farm.
* **VM0042 v2.2 explicitly allows splitting a field:** *"Depending on the size of the agricultural fields or paddocks, strata may span numerous fields/paddocks, or one field/paddock may be divided into several strata."* (§8.2.1.2, p.29). Its Appendix 6 example design uses **"Within-field strata, designed based on physical (e.g., topographic indices) or soil data (e.g., clay content)"** (p.159).
* **The FAO GSOC-MRV protocol goes further. Every intervention area must contain at least three strata** (*"A minimum of three strata shall be included in each IA"*, Annex 3, pp.89–90). A single-farm intervention area is therefore always split.
* **Management-driven methodologies are field-based, not farm-based.** VM0051 (rice) says *"A stratum must consist of fields that share the same cultivation pattern"* and *"Areas differing in any of these parameters must be assigned to separate strata"* (App. 1, p.76). The unit there is the **field** (the management unit), not the farm. A farm with an AWD part and a continuously flooded part must sit in two strata.
* **Making each whole farm one stratum, or grouping whole farms, is not a rule anywhere.** It is a simplification. It is acceptable only where each farm really is homogeneous in the stratification factors. It is wrong (or at least inefficient and hard to defend) when a farm spans soil types, slope classes, water regimes, or areas with and without the practice.
* **Strata must be stable within a monitoring period.** They may be refined between sampling campaigns (SSA Handbook §3.2.2; CCTS FR05.002 ¶13(b)). Every verification must report strata areas, maps and the points in each stratum (VM0042 §8.2.1.2; SEP Table 6.2).

## 2. Synthesis: what is valid, and what is best practice?

### 2.1 What the unit of stratification is

| Methodology family | Stratification unit | Farm as a unit? |
|---|---|---|
| SOC, measure-based (VM0042, SSA, GS 402 Approach 1, FAO, CAR SEP) | **Area polygon** inside a QU / MU / IA, from SOC maps and covariates. Can cross field and farm lines and can cut through fields. | Farm = enrolment, data and cluster unit only. |
| Management-defined (VM0051 rice, GS 402 modules for management criteria, CAR SEP accounting) | **Field / management unit**, grouped by management pattern. Areas that differ in a mandatory factor go to different strata. | Only if the whole farm is managed one way. |
| Tree biomass (CCTS/CDM AR, VM0047 area-based) | Parcels grouped, **or** remote-sensing polygons (crown cover). Re-stratified ex post. | No. |
| VM0047 census-based | **Planting-unit list** (e.g., annual cohort). Not spatial. | N/A |
| IPCC inventory | Climate × soil × management × land use cells. | No. |

### 2.2 Is "whole farm = one stratum" acceptable, required, or a simplification?

* **Required?** No. No methodology reviewed requires strata to be made of whole farms. VM0042 explicitly allows splitting fields (p.29). FAO **requires** at least three strata inside every intervention area (Annex 3). The SSA Handbook treats farms and fields as **clusters for multistage sampling**, which is a different idea from strata (App. 7.6).
* **Acceptable?** Only as a **special case** where each farm (or group of farms) really is homogeneous in every stratification factor the methodology lists, and the farm is managed as one unit. Under VM0051 the test is strict: any difference in water regime, organic amendment, biochar or methanotrophs inside a farm forces a split (App. 1, p.76). Under VM0042 and GS 402 you must "report the factors used in stratification and how strata were developed" (VM0042 p.29). "We grouped whole farms" is a defensible answer only if the soil and topography data show the farms are homogeneous.
* **A simplification?** Yes. It costs precision. Stratification aims for strata "more homogenous than the project area in its entirety" (VM0042 p.29). Putting a farm that spans two soil types into one stratum inflates within-stratum variance and therefore the uncertainty deduction (SSA §3.2, design effect). It can also misrepresent area weights.

### 2.3 When within-farm strata are needed (or strongly advisable)

| Situation | Why | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Farm crosses two soil types or texture classes | Soil type and texture are core stratification factors. VM0051 requires a stratum's mean texture to fall in one FAO textural class. FAO says material differences in soil type "shall trigger delineation of separate IAs". | VM0042 p.29; GS SOC FM §6; VM0051 Table 4; FAO §4.1 |
| Strong slope or landform contrast (e.g., terrace vs valley floor), drainage, flooding | Topography, landform, drainage and wetness are listed factors. | VM0042 p.29 / Table 7; SSA p.16; Winrock §6.3; SEP Table 6.2; FAO §4.1 |
| **Partial practice adoption** (cover crops or no-till on some fields only) | Practice is a stratification factor (GS 402.6 subdivides strata by cover crop type). Under VM0042 the baseline/project schedule of activities is per QU (e.g., per field). Fields without the practice are either not in the project or in a different QU/stratum. | GS 402.6 §8.1.4; VM0042 p.5; SSA p.16 ("practice changes [...] should be considered") |
| Part irrigated / part rainfed; rice part AWD / part continuously flooded | Irrigation and water regime are mandatory (VM0051) or listed (IPCC, Winrock) factors. | VM0051 App. 1; IPCC Ch.5 pp.5.17–5.18; Winrock §6.3 |
| Different quantification approach on parts of a farm | Must be stratified and accounted separately. | VM0042 p.20 |
| Large farm with known internal SOC gradient (yield, NDVI, EC maps) | Directed stratification inside the IA. | FAO Annex 3; SSA §3.2 |
| Part of a farm ineligible (Histosol, wetland, road, recent clearing) | Exclude from project area. The field or stratum boundary is redrawn. | CAR SEP §2.2.2; FAO §4.2 |
| Tree projects with parcels harvested or burned | Re-stratify ex post. | CCTS FR05.002 ¶13(b); BM-T-AR-0004 §4.10 |

### 2.4 Rules for boundaries, size, points, and changes (consolidated)

* **Boundaries:** geographic polygons mapped in GIS, reported with area at every verification (VM0042 p.29; SEP Table 6.2; VCS Standard §3.10.1). Field boundaries "should be taken into account" (VM0042 p.30), but strata may cut through fields. Samples stay ≥10 m from field boundaries and other artefacts (SSA p.18).
* **Minimum size:** none stated anywhere. It is limited in practice by the samples needed: ≥3 points (SEP), ≥3 composites (FAO), 3–5 composites (VM0042), ≥3 control sites plus ≥3 sample units (VM0051 QA2), and ≥2 for a variance estimate (CDM AR). The SSA Handbook warns that maps coarser than 100 m are unsuitable inside fields, which sets a practical lower bound on polygon detail.
* **Points per stratum:** placed randomly and reproducibly within each stratum (SSA p.18), allocated proportionally or by Neyman (SSA Table 4). VM0042 also says "on a proportional basis by area" (p.63). Plan a buffer for points that become inaccessible (SSA §3.3.1).
* **Change over time:** fixed within a monitoring period. May be refined before each remeasurement. Must be revised when QUs or instances change. Paired designs must revisit earlier units (SSA §3.2.2). VM0042: "Defined strata should remain stable over time" (p.30). VM0047 SOC: the design should be held constant, and any change documented to Verra and the VVB (p.53). CCTS AR: re-stratify ex post when disturbance alters biomass (¶13(b)). IPCC: avoid time-series inconsistency (pp.2.57–2.58).

## 3. Practical implications for the platform

### 3.1 Current model (as found in `backend/app/models/mrv.py` and `app/services/sampling_service.py`)

* `ProjectStratum.geometry` / `area_hectares` = SQL Server union of member farms' current boundaries (`_set_farms` → `gis.union_of_boundaries`).
* `StratumFarm(stratum_id, farm_id, farm_boundary_id)`, with the rule `"A farm can belong to only one current stratum."` (`FARM_ALREADY_STRATIFIED`).
* `generate_points` draws candidates in the bbox of the member farm boundaries and keeps those inside a stratum farm (`points_in_boundaries` over `farm_boundary_ids`). Each `SamplingPoint` carries `farm_id` and `farm_boundary_id`.
* There is no field or parcel entity below the farm.

This setup handles the special case well (homogeneous farms grouped by declared characteristics). It cannot represent VM0042's "one field/paddock may be divided into several strata", FAO's ≥3 strata per IA, VM0051's "areas differing [...] must be assigned to separate strata" inside a farm, or GS 402.6 subdivision by cover crop.

### 3.2 What would need to change

1. **The stratum geometry becomes the primary object, not a derived union.**
   * Add `source` values such as `DRAWN` (user polygon) and `DERIVED` (from a raster or vector layer: soil map unit, slope class, SoilGrids SOC class, k-means cluster), alongside `FARM_GROUPING`.
   * Store `geometry` as authored, then **clip to the project area / enrolled farm boundaries** (`STIntersection`). Validate: valid polygon, inside the project boundary, and **no overlap between current PROJECT strata of the same QU**, because strata must be non-overlapping and exhaustive (SSA §2.3). Also flag enrolled area that no stratum covers (coverage gap = Σ farm area − Σ stratum area within the QU).
   * Keep `criteria` JSON, but record the **input layers and versions** used ("best available data", "report the factors used", VM0042 p.29).

2. **Many-to-many farm↔stratum with area shares (replacing the one-farm-one-stratum rule).**
   * Replace the `FARM_ALREADY_STRATIFIED` rule with a geometric non-overlap rule.
   * Make `StratumFarm` a **farm-stratum intersection** table: `(stratum_id, farm_id, farm_boundary_id, intersection_geometry, intersection_area_ha, share_of_farm, share_of_stratum)`, computed by SQL Server at save or approval and frozen with the stratum version.
   * Use these intersections for (a) area weights `A_h` (the sum of pieces must equal the stratum area), (b) per-farm attribution of credits (SEP requires results allocated back to fields, and VM0042 QU-level claims still need per-farm payouts per SSA §7.7), and (c) collector logistics and land-owner consent.

3. **Optional sub-farm management units (fields/plots).**
   * Methodologies keyed to management (VM0051, VM0042 schedule of activities "for each field", CAR SEP "same crop throughout each field") need a **field/plot entity under the farm**, with its own versioned boundary. Activity data (practices, water regime, inputs) are then recorded per field, or per farm with an "applies to whole farm" flag.
   * Strata for VM0051 are then groups of **fields** (not farms). Strata for VM0042 SOC are polygons that may cut fields.
   * Where activity data exist only per farm, follow SEP: *"those data may be applied to all units within the relevant field"*. That is, inherit farm/field-level practice data down to each stratum piece and each point. Make the inheritance explicit in calculation inputs (`STRATUM_CHARACTERISTIC` source = `FARM_DATA`, with the farm id and share).
   * **Consistency check:** if a stratum's criteria include a management factor (tillage, cover crop, water regime), every farm or field intersecting it must report that value for the period. Otherwise the stratum is not homogeneous and must be split. This is VM0051's "must be assigned to separate strata" check.

4. **Point generation inside the stratum polygon.**
   * Sample within `ProjectStratum.geometry` (clipped), not within the farm union. Test candidates with `STContains` against the stratum geometry, apply a **negative buffer of 10 m** from field/farm boundaries, roads and artefacts (SSA p.18), and keep the minimum spacing.
   * Each point still gets `farm_id` / `farm_boundary_id` (and `field_id` if added) by point-in-polygon lookup, for access, consent and activity-data lookup.
   * Allocation: offer proportional-by-area (VM0042 p.63) and Neyman (SSA Table 4) on top of manual counts, and **enforce methodology minimums per stratum**: VM0042 3–5 composites (should), SEP ≥3 points, FAO ≥3 composites and ≥3 strata per IA, VM0051 ≥3 sample units plus ≥3 control sites.
   * Add a warning for strata too small to hold the required points at the configured spacing. This replaces the current `INSUFFICIENT_AREA` failure, which is all users see today.

5. **Versioning and re-stratification rules.**
   * Freeze strata for the duration of a monitoring period. A sampling design version must reference stratum versions that stay unchanged until the period closes (SSA §3.2.2; VM0042 p.30).
   * Allow a new stratum version only between periods, with `change_reason` and a re-stratification type (refinement, QU change from added or removed farms, disturbance (CCTS), approach change).
   * For **paired designs** (VM0042 QA1 true-up), carry earlier points forward into the new strata: re-assign by point-in-polygon, flag lost pairs, and support buffer points (SSA §3.3.1, §3.4).
   * Recompute farm-stratum intersections when a farm boundary version changes. Under VM0042, a farm leaving mid-period means retrospective adjustment (SSA §3.4).
   * Export the per-verification annex: stratum id, area, polygon (GeoJSON/KML), points with coordinates (intended and actual), factors used (VM0042 p.29; VM0051 p.41; SEP Table 6.2).

6. **Hierarchy: QU → stratum.**
   * Add an explicit **quantification unit** (VM0042/VM0051), MU (GS) or IA (FAO) level above strata. Strata are subsets of exactly one QU. VM0042 says strata at the "lowest level of quantification unit" (p.29).
   * A QU may be a field, a farm, a landowner, a region, or (VM0051) a stratum itself.
   * Credits are claimed per QU (SSA §7.7). Payouts to farms come from intersection shares or point/model allocation.

### 3.3 Methodology-specific pitfalls

| Methodology | Pitfall | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| **VM0042 control sites (QA2)** | "at least one control site per stratum, or the control site must be divided into the same strata as the corresponding quantification unit" (p.25). With sub-farm strata, the control site (a non-participant farm) must either be split by the **same stratification layers**, or each control polygon must be linked to one project stratum. Control sites must meet Table 7 similarity criteria and lie within 250 km. | Allow CONTROL strata to be **derived by applying the same stratification rule** to the control farm geometry. Check the one-to-one or one-to-many link per project stratum. The current `linked_stratum_record_ids` can stay, but validation must be per stratum, not per farm. |
| **VM0042 QA1 true-up** | Paired points must be revisited. Re-stratifying moves points between strata. Model runs are per point with per-point management data (p.62). | Keep point identity across stratum versions. Store management inputs resolved per point (from field/farm). |
| **VM0042 / VM0051 approach split** | Areas on different quantification approaches must be separate strata (VM0042 p.20). In VM0051, biochar or methanotroph fields must use QA2. | Add a validation that no stratum mixes approaches. Within-farm splits are therefore needed whenever only part of a farm uses QA2. |
| **VM0051 QU vs stratum** | A QU "may be a stratum, a cluster of fields" (p.9). The stratification unit is the **field**, and any difference in water regime, amendment, biochar or methanotroph across a farm forces separate strata. Static factors are fixed at validation, dynamic factors may change. QA2 needs ≥3 control sites and ≥3 sample units **per stratum**. | Needs a field entity, or at least sub-farm polygons with management attributes. Re-check dynamic factors each season: a field that changes water regime changes stratum. |
| **VM0047 census instances** | Census-based "strata" are **lists of planting units** (e.g., annual cohorts), not polygons. Census and area-based instances must be spatially separate (10 m buffer, fixed at start). The area-based SOC design must be constant, with any change documented. | Do not force polygon strata on census instances. Model census strata as cohort lists. Enforce the 10 m separation and non-overlap of the two accounting boundaries. |
| **VM0047 performance benchmark** | Project plots are equal-sized 0.01–10 ha remote-sensing units per annual cohort, ≥75% inside the area. Strata may have <30 plots only if the total is ≥30 and each stratum has its own benchmark. | Generate this grid from the cohort polygon, separate from soil strata. |
| **GS 402 / modules** | MUs combine soil, climate, management and inputs. 402.6 requires subdividing baseline bare-land strata by cover crop type. Baseline data are per **field within a stratum**. Different approaches per stratum are allowed. | Field-level activity data with a link to the MU. Allow per-MU approach and model selection (402.7 §5.2.2). |
| **FAO GSOC** | ≥3 strata per IA, even for a single homogeneous field (equal-area split if no data). | A "split polygon into k equal-area strata" generator (grid or k-means on coordinates). |
| **CAR SEP** | Same crop throughout each field. Results must be allocated back to fields and sum to the project total. ≥3 points per stratum. | Field-level allocation table, built from the intersections above. |
| **CCTS AR / CDM AR-TOOL14** | Ex-post re-stratification after harvest, fire or poor growth. Strata may be drawn from crown-cover imagery. | Support DERIVED strata from imagery. Version per verification. |

### 3.4 Minimal migration path

1. Keep `FARM_GROUPING` as one way to build a stratum polygon (a whole-farm union). Existing projects remain valid where farms are homogeneous.
2. Add `DRAWN` / `DERIVED` geometry and change point generation to sample inside the stratum geometry. This is the change with the most value.
3. Drop the farm-uniqueness rule in favour of geometric non-overlap plus coverage checks, and store farm-stratum intersection areas.
4. Add a QU level and an optional field/plot entity, and route activity data through intersections.
5. Add methodology-specific validators (minimum points per stratum, control-site pairing per stratum, VM0051 mandatory-factor homogeneity, census and area separation for VM0047).
