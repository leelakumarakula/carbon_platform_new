## 1. Structure

### 1.1 Summary (p.4)
- **Additionality:** Activity Method (positive list), plus regulatory surplus and an investment analysis (v1.2).
- **Crediting baseline:** Project Method.
- **Project activity has three parts:** (1) source waste biomass, (2) produce biochar, (3) use the biochar in a soil or non-soil application.
- **Greenfield only:** the project must install and operate a **new (greenfield)** biochar production facility (or facilities).
- **What is credited:** **only biochar that is actually used** in eligible soil or non-soil applications.
- **Project start date:** the first instance of biochar production.
- **Baseline:** the waste biomass is left to decay or is burned for non-energy purposes. Baseline emissions at sourcing are conservatively **zero**.

### 1.2 Applicability conditions (Section 4, pp.8-14)

**Technological scope**
1. Biochar is produced from eligible waste biomass by a thermochemical process: pyrolysis, gasification, or biomass boilers (footnote 7: these terms are used interchangeably). The biochar is then applied to an end-use (soil or non-soil).
   - **Torrefaction and hydrothermal carbonization are excluded** (fn 8).
2. Both **low and high technology production facilities** are eligible (definitions in Section 3).
3. Producers must have a health and safety program that protects workers from airborne pollutants.

**Feedstock (condition 4, Table 1, pp.9-12).** The feedstock must:
- (a) be purely biogenic waste biomass, not purpose-grown;
- (b) otherwise have been left to decay or burned for non-energy purposes (see Appendix 2);
- (c) **not be imported from other countries**;
- (d) meet the Table 1 sustainability criteria.

**Table 1: feedstock categories and key criteria (the example lists are non-exhaustive)**

| Category | Sustainability criteria | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Agricultural waste biomass | Taken from fields (not from a centralized processing operation): documentation that there is no decline in soil C or crop productivity, or that the residues were burned in the baseline without energy recovery. **Without documentation, removal is limited to ≤50% of total residues.** | Tree, vine and shrub prunings; harvest residues (straw, leaves, stalks, husk, pomace, kernels); fruit and vegetable residues |
| Food processing residues | Must come from food processing facilities. Residue production per unit of output must not have increased in order to make biochar. | Washing, cleaning, peeling, centrifugation and separation residues; expired food; coffee, tea, cocoa and tobacco processing residues |
| Forestry and other wood processing | Forest-sourced wood (as opposed to mill by-products) must be proven sustainable and not cause deforestation or degradation (management plan, PEFC/FSC, or CDM EB23 Annex 18 renewable biomass). Processed timber used for soil application must contain no paint, solvents or contaminants. | Off-cuts, sawdust; forest-industry by-products (pallets, wood waste); wildfire-fuel-reduction thinnings and overstocked areas; non-merchantable prunings and thinnings; diseased trees; burned woody biomass; wastepaper and cardboard |
| Recycling economy | Must meet heavy metal and contaminant thresholds (IBI/EBC). Only the non-fossil fraction of industrial or municipal waste. | Green cuttings and municipal green waste; biosolids from wastewater treatment; paper mill sludge |
| Aquaculture plants | Must be aquaculture by-products. Invasive species (e.g., water hyacinth) must not have been purposely introduced. | Seaweed, algae waste, aquatic invasive plants (Plantae only) |
| Animal manure | Must be a by-product of animal husbandry. | Swine, cattle, horse and poultry waste |
| High-carbon fly ash (HCFA) from biomass | HCFA must be ≤5% of the bioenergy facility's annual waste biomass throughput. An "affirmative" extraction technology must be used. No fossil fuel may replace the lost calorific value. The final material must meet IBI. | By-products of cogeneration facilities |

**Other feedstock and production conditions**
5. The biochar must comply with the latest **IBI Biochar Testing Guidelines** or **EBC Production Guidelines**.
6. Transport rule (wording reproduced as in the source): feedstock and biochar "may be transported via ships, boats, and vehicles other than road transportation up to a distance of 200 km. However, it must only be transported by vehicles (i.e., road transportation) for distances more than 200 km as defined under CDM TOOL12". See Ambiguity A13.
7. Mineral additives (lime, rock minerals, ash) may make up **up to 10% of mass**. Above 10% on a dry weight basis, lab tests must show the product meets IBI/EBC contaminant thresholds.
8. Biomass certification (RSB, ISCC, EU RED Annex IX, CORSIA, and similar) may be used as evidence of sustainability.

**End-use criteria**
9. The biochar must be **used within one year of its production**. Permanence is calculated over 100 years.
10. **Soil application:**
    - Allowed on land other than wetlands: cropland, grassland, vegetated urban soils, forest.
    - Surface application: the biochar **must be mixed** with compost, manure or digestate.
    - Subsurface application: the biochar may be applied alone or mixed.
    - The biochar must (a) meet IBI/EBC or national contaminant rules and (b) have **H:C_org (molar) ≤ 0.7**.
11. **Non-soil applications** (cement, asphalt, other long-term storage) are allowed **only for biochar from high technology facilities**.
12. Long-lived products must be demonstrated with credible evidence (lab results, peer-reviewed papers, third-party product assessment, decay-rate analysis), **including the product lifetime**. Products must meet national or international product standards (e.g., ACI).

**Biochar for non-soil use** must be **≥50% carbon by dry weight** (Definitions, p.8; charcoal is excluded).

**Not applicable when** (conditions 13-15, p.14):
- the biochar is used for energy or as a fuel (a substitute for charcoal or coke);
- substantial oxidation occurs (burning, reduction agent in steel making, activated carbon, other fossil-fuel-intensive uses);
- a non-soil application loses more than 50% of the carbon (dry weight basis).

### 1.3 Key definitions (Section 3, pp.6-8)
- **High technology production facility.** The facility must meet **all** of these conditions (from the EBC Production Guidelines):
  - (a) pyrolysis gases are recovered or combusted, with none escaping;
  - (b) **≥70% of the pyrolysis heat energy is used** (allowing for transfer inefficiencies; demonstrated with the feedstock LHV and the biochar yield; monitored as WS_P);
  - (c) pollution controls (e.g., a thermal oxidizer) meet local, national or international thresholds (the strictest applies);
  - (d) **production temperature is measured and reported**.
  - If any condition fails, the facility is **low technology** (p.7, p.21).
- **Dry weight basis.** IBI guidelines / ASTM D1762-84 for biochar. ASTM D4442-20 for feedstock moisture. Oven-dry at 102-105 °C (p.6).
- **Organic carbon content.** Mass fraction (stated "in percent") of organic carbon permanently stored, on a biochar dry weight basis (p.8).
- **Long-lived biochar products.** Non-soil storage where the average product life is proven from peer-reviewed literature, and the biochar is **not combusted at end of life** (pp.7-8).
- **Freight.** Trips grouped by the same vehicle class and the same origin-destination pair (p.7).

### 1.4 Project boundary (Section 5, Table 2, pp.14-17)

**Spatial boundary:** the sourcing areas, the production facility (or facilities), and the application sites.

| Stage | Source | CO2 | CH4 | N2O | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Feedstock production | No | No | No | Renewable waste biomass |
| Baseline | Feedstock transportation | **Yes** | No | No | CO2 only, per TOOL12; CH4 de minimis if <200 km |
| Baseline | Combustion, aerobic or anaerobic decay of feedstock | No | No | No | Excluded; baseline = 0 (conservative) |
| Project | Feedstock production | No | No | No | No purpose-grown crops |
| Project | Pyrolysis, high technology | No | No | No | Pollution controls; de minimis |
| Project | Pyrolysis, low technology | No | **Yes** | No | Default CH4 value (Cornelissen et al. 2016) |
| Project | Electricity and/or fossil fuel for the thermochemical process | **Yes** | No | No | TOOL03/TOOL05 |
| Project | Biochar transportation | **Yes** | No | No | TOOL12; CH4 de minimis if <200 km |
| Project | Pre-treatment of feedstock (grinding, drying) | **Yes** | No | No | TOOL03/TOOL05 |
| Project | Biochar application (preparation for final use) | **Yes** | No | No | TOOL03/TOOL05 |

### 1.5 Baseline (Section 6, p.17; Section 8.1, pp.19-20)
- **Baseline scenario:** the waste biomass is left to decay or burned for non-energy purposes. Credible evidence is required: government records, waste facility or production facility records, or else literature, regional surveys, or the proponent's own survey.
- **Baseline emissions:**
  - BE_SS,y = 0 at sourcing.
  - Production-stage and application-stage baselines are zero (no biochar is produced).

### 1.6 Additionality (Section 7, pp.17-18; Appendix 1, pp.46-49)
- **Step 1:** regulatory surplus (VCS Standard and VCS Methodology Requirements).
- **Step 2:** positive list. Meeting all applicability conditions satisfies it. It was established by activity penetration (Option A); AP_y = 0.06% < 5% (Eq. 16).
- **Step 3 (new in v1.2):** investment analysis per VT0008, using investment comparison (Option 1) or benchmark (Option 2).
- Where the activity has been commercially available for less than 3 years, barriers must also be shown (p.49).

### 1.7 Quantification steps (Section 8)
1. Sourcing stage: ER_SS,y = BE_SS,y − PE_SS,y = 0 − 0 (Eq. 14).
2. For each facility p, decide **high tech (Option P.1)** or **low tech (Option P.2)**.
3. For each biochar type t (defined by feedstock) and application type k: compute CC_t,k,y = Σ_p M × F_Cp × PR_de,k (Eq. 2 or Eq. 6).
4. Facility production emissions: P_ED (Eq. 4/8) + P_EP (high tech = 0; low tech Eq. 9) + P_EC (Eq. 5/10). Allocate to used biochar with Eq. 3/7.
5. ER_PS,y (Eq. 1).
6. Application-stage emissions PE_AS,y (Eq. 11, 12; E_ap = 0).
7. Leakage LE_y (Eq. 13): transport only, counted only above 200 km (round trip), using TOOL12.
8. ER_y = ER_SS,y + ER_PS,y − PE_AS,y − LE_y (Eq. 15).

### Eq. 1: Removals at the production stage (p.20) [v1.1 p.20] CONFIRMED

Plain: `ER_PS,y = Σ_t ( ( Σ_k CC_t,k,y × 44/12 ) − ( Σ_p PE_PS,t,p,y ) )`

LaTeX: `ER_{PS,y} = \sum_{t}\left(\left(\sum_{k} CC_{t,k,y} \times \frac{44}{12}\right) - \left(\sum_{p} PE_{PS,t,p,y}\right)\right)`

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| ER_PS,y | GHG emission removals at the production stage in year y | tCO2e |
| CC_t,k,y | Organic carbon content on a dry weight basis for biochar type t used for application type k in year y (this is a mass of persistent C) | t C |
| PE_PS,t,p,y | Project emissions at the production stage for biochar type t at facility p in year y | tCO2e |

Note: Eq. 3/7 define PE_PS,**p**,y, with no t index. See Ambiguity A1.

### Eq. 2: Persistent organic carbon, high tech (Option P.1) (p.21) [v1.1 p.21] CONFIRMED

Plain: `CC_t,k,y = Σ_p ( M_t,k,p,y × F_Cp,t,p × PR_de,k )`

LaTeX: `CC_{t,k,y} = \sum_{p}\left(M_{t,k,p,y} \times F_{Cp,t,p} \times PR_{de,k}\right)`

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| CC_t,k,y | Organic C content (dry basis) of biochar type t used for application k in year y. Biochar type = the feedstock used. | t |
| M_t,k,p,y | Dry-basis mass of biochar type t for application k produced at facility p in year y | t (dry) |

### Eq. 3: Production-stage project emissions, high tech (p.22) [v1.1 p.22] CONFIRMED

Plain: `PE_PS,p,y = ( P_ED,p,y + P_EP,p,y + P_EC,p,y ) × ( Σ_t Σ_k M_t,k,p,y ) / M_p,y`

LaTeX: `PE_{PS,p,y} = \left(P_{ED,p,y} + P_{EP,p,y} + P_{EC,p,y}\right) \times \frac{\sum_{t}\sum_{k} M_{t,k,p,y}}{M_{p,y}}`

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| PE_PS,p,y | Project emissions at the production stage, facility p, year y | tCO2e |
| P_ED,p,y | Emissions from pre-treatment of waste biomass | tCO2e |
| P_EP,p,y | Emissions from converting biomass to biochar (pyrolysis) | tCO2e |
| P_EC,p,y | Emissions from auxiliary energy used for pyrolysis | tCO2e |
| M_t,k,p,y | Dry mass of biochar type t for application k from facility p | t |
| M_p,y | **Total** dry mass of biochar produced at facility p in year y | t |

The source typesets the symbols as P_ED, P_EP, P_EC: a capital P with subscripts "ED", "EP", "EC". Other parts of the document write them as "PE_D", "PE_P", "PE_C". They are the same variables.

### Eq. 4: Pre-treatment emissions, high tech (p.23) [v1.1 p.23] CONFIRMED

Plain: `P_ED,p,y = P_EDE,p,y + P_EDF,p,y`

LaTeX: `P_{ED,p,y} = P_{EDE,p,y} + P_{EDF,p,y}`

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |

| P_EDE,p,y | Grid electricity used for pre-treatment; **calculated per CDM TOOL05** | tCO2e |

**Rule:** if the energy source is renewable, P_ED,p,y = 0. Pre-treatment covers agglomeration, homogenization, pelletizing, in-field preparation and drying.

### Eq. 6: Persistent organic carbon, low tech (Option P.2) (p.25) [v1.1 p.24] CONFIRMED

LaTeX: `CC_{t,k,y} = \sum_{p}\left(M_{t,k,p,y} \times F_{Cp,t,p} \times PR_{de,k}\right)`

### Eq. 9: Pyrolysis CH4, low tech (p.27) [v1.1 p.27] CONFIRMED

### Eq. 16: Activity penetration (Appendix 1, p.46; worked on p.49) [v1.1 p.47; the form is simple]

Plain: `AP_y = OA_y / MAP_y × 100`

LaTeX: `AP_{y} = \frac{OA_{y}}{MAP_{y}} \times 100`

Worked values:
- OA_y = 773,787 t biochar (2021 estimate). This is 170,000 t (IBI 2015 ×2) grown at 10%/yr from 2016-2021 to 273,787 t, plus 500,000 t for China.
- MAP_y = 1,369,486,910 t. This is 269,486,910 t wood residues (336,858,637 m³ ÷ 1.25 m³/t) plus 1.1 billion t crop residues (10% of 11.9 billion t of crops).
- AP_y = 0.06% < 5%. My check: 773,787 / 1,369,486,910 × 100 = 0.0565%.

This equation is for additionality only and is not needed in the per-batch engine.

### Reference implementation (per year y; my composition of the equations above)

```
for each facility p:
   M_p      = total dry biochar produced (all uses, incl. unused/unsold)
   M_used_p = Σ_t Σ_k M[t,k,p]                       # biochar used in eligible applications
   P_ED = 0 if renewable else P_EDE(TOOL05) + P_EDF(TOOL03)
   P_EP = 0 if high_tech else Σ_t Σ_k Fe × GWP_CH4 × M[t,k,p]
   P_EC = 0 if renewable else P_ECE(TOOL05) + P_ECF(TOOL03)
   PE_PS[p] = (P_ED + P_EP + P_EC) × M_used_p / M_p
CC[t,k] = Σ_p M[t,k,p] × FCp[t,p] × PRde[k,(p)]
ER_PS = Σ_t Σ_k CC[t,k] × 44/12 − Σ_p PE_PS[p]          # see A1 for t-indexing
PE_AS = Σ_k Σ_t (E_P[t,k] + 0)
LE = 0 + 0 + LE_ts(TOOL12, if round trip ≥200 km) + LE_tap(TOOL12, if round trip ≥200 km)
ER_y = 0 + ER_PS − PE_AS − LE
```

Note on PR_de: in practice it depends on the facility (through T_prod, or tech level) and on k. The source indexes it by k only (see A3).

## 3. Default values and tables

### 4.3 Transport legs (feedstock and biochar)

Each leg needs: origin, destination, **one-way and round-trip distance (km)**, vehicle class, mode (road/ship/other), freight mass (t), and number of trips. ### 4.4 Facility and period energy

- **Pre-treatment:** grid electricity (MWh, with grid EF per TOOL05) and fossil fuel by type (quantity, NCV, EF per TOOL03). Plus a renewable flag.

- **Biochar post-processing (grinding/sieving) by application k:** the same fields.
- **WS_P:** fraction of waste heat used (%), annual (TOOL09) → high tech qualification (p.40).
- **High tech evidence:** gas recovery or combustion, emission controls compliant, temperature measured.

For each feedstock category:

## 5. Uncertainty, buffer/permanence, VCU computation

- **Uncertainty:**
  - VM0044 has **no uncertainty quantification or deduction procedure**: no confidence intervals and no discounts.
  - Conservativeness is built in through defaults: lowest F_Cp for mixed feedstocks, the lower PR_de from the literature, 0.56 when the temperature is unknown, 0.049 for unknown kilns, and zero baselines.
  - QA/QC is procedural (pp.42-43).
  - Note: VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0 §2.4 asks methodologies to assess uncertainty. VM0044 v1.2 predates it (see A15).
- **Buffer / non-permanence:**
  - VM0044 is **sectoral scope 13 (WHD), not AFOLU**. It **does not use the AFOLU Non-Permanence Risk Tool or the AFOLU pooled buffer**. VCS Standard v5.0 §2.4 / §3.2.8-3.2.16 apply the NPRT and buffer to AFOLU and GCS projects.
  - Permanence is handled **inside the calculation** through the 100-year decay factor PR_de,k.
  - Section 8.4 (pp.31-33) concludes that **reversal risk is negligible** for natural risks (fire, erosion: mitigated by subsurface incorporation and mixing for surface use) and for non-natural risks (social, management or financial). **No buffer deduction.**
- **VCU computation:**
  - VCUs for vintage/period = ER_y (Eq. 15), in tCO2e. One VCU = 1 tCO2e (VCS Program Definitions v5.0).
  - The credits are removals: the 44/12 × CC term, net of emissions.
  - **Rounding:** VM0044 states **no rounding rule**. VCUs are issued as whole units (1 t each). Rounding down to an integer at issuance is the conventional conservative choice, but this is a platform/registry decision, not a methodology rule.
  - If ER_y is negative (emissions exceed removals), the methodology says nothing about carry-forward (see A14).

## 6. Ambiguities and issues (with page refs)

- **A1 (Eq. 1 vs Eq. 3/7, pp.20, 22, 26).**
  - The problem: Eq. 1 sums PE_PS,**t**,p,y over p inside the sum over t. But Eq. 3/7 define PE_PS,**p**,y with no t index; it is already scaled by the share of *all* used types (Σ_t Σ_k M / M_p).
  - Literal reading: subtracting PE_PS,p,y once for each t would double count when a facility makes more than one biochar type.
  - **[RECONSTRUCTED] recommended implementation:** subtract Σ_p PE_PS,p,y once in total. This is equivalent to defining PE_PS,t,p,y = (P_ED+P_EP+P_EC) × Σ_k M_t,k,p,y / M_p,y.
- **A2 (F_Cp unit, pp.22, 25, 34, 38).** The unit is given as "percent (%)", but the Table 4 values are fractions (0.77) and Eq. 2 needs a fraction. Store percent and divide by 100, or store a fraction. Validate inputs above 1.
- **A3 (PR_de indexing, p.22).** PR_de is indexed by application k only, but it depends on facility temperature or technology level. Implement it as PR_de(k, p or batch). T_prod is an **annual average** per facility (p.39), so a per-batch temperature class is not strictly what the methodology specifies.
- **A4 (Table 3 boundaries, p.22).**
  - 450 °C falls in both "450–600" and "350–450". 600 °C is in "450–600" (>600 is high).
  - Below 350 °C is not covered.
  - Conservative implementation: T > 600 → 0.89; 450 < T ≤ 600 → 0.80; 350 ≤ T ≤ 450 → 0.65 (450 goes to the lower class); T < 350 → not covered by the table, so flag it (0.56 is the only other default).
- **A5 (Typos carried from v1.1).**
  - Eq. 7 "Where" block lists P_ED twice; the second is P_EC (p.26).
  - The PR_de parameter table says "For high technology production facilities, project proponents must use **Table 4**" (p.34). It should be **Table 3** (Table 4 is F_Cp).
  - The T_prod comments say "use the conservative values indicated in **Equation 7**" (p.39). Presumably this means Table 4 / 0.56.
  - Section 8.4 cites "applicability conditions 5 and 7" for the end-use restriction and "condition 5" for mixing at surface application (pp.31-32). The correct conditions are 9-14 and 10.
- **A6 (Indices, pp.28-29).** Eq. 11 uses E_P,t,k,y, but Eq. 12 defines E_P,k,y (no t). Allocate processing energy across types t by mass, or compute it per k and sum once.
- **A7 (Low tech PR_de 0.56, pp.25 vs 35).**
  - Eq. 6 text: use 0.56 "where pyrolysis temperature is unknown".
  - Parameter table: "For low technology production facilities, project proponents must use a conservative default value of 0.56", with no condition.
  - It is unclear whether a low tech facility with a measured T_prod may use Table 3. The conservative choice is 0.56 for all low tech.
- **A8 (Eq. 9 scaling, pp.26-27).**
  - P_EP is computed on the **used** biochar mass (Σ M_t,k,p,y), then multiplied again in Eq. 7 by Σ M_t,k,p,y / M_p,y.
  - Result: CH4 for used biochar is scaled down twice, and CH4 from biochar that is produced but not used is ignored.
  - The literal reading is implemented above. A conservative alternative is F_e × GWP × M_p,y inside Eq. 7. Flag for validator confirmation.
- **A9 (H:C_org threshold, p.13 vs p.40).** Condition 10b says "**less than or equal to** 0.7". The H:C_org parameter table says "less than 0.7". Use ≤ 0.7 (the applicability condition governs). H:C_org is an eligibility gate only, not a factor in any equation.
- **A10 (Monitoring frequency mismatch, pp.38, 40).** F_Cp is tested annually or on a material change, while H:C_org is tested **every batch**. A per-batch H:C_org analysis normally produces C_org as well. Whether a per-batch C_org must replace the annual F_Cp is not stated.
- **A11 (Ex-ante F_Cp, p.34).** At validation, both tech types may use Table 4 defaults. High tech ex-post must use lab values (p.21).
- **A12 (Table 2 vs Section 8.3, pp.15-16, 29-31).**
  - Table 2 lists "Feedstock transportation" CO2 under **Baseline** = Yes, and "Biochar transportation" as a project source. Section 8 quantifies both as **leakage** (LE_ts, LE_tap).
  - Treat both as leakage (Eq. 13). The baseline remains zero.
  - Table 2 also says "Biochar application" CO2 = Yes, but E_ap is fixed at 0. Only processing E_P is quantified.
- **A13 (200 km rules, p.12 vs pp.30-31).**
  - Condition 6 is garbled in wording. It appears to say non-road modes are allowed up to 200 km and only road vehicles beyond 200 km. Taken literally, that inverts the usual intent.
  - Leakage: emissions are counted only if the distance is ">200 km" (TOOL16), and the result is zero if the **round trip** is "<200 km". Exactly 200 km is unspecified.
  - Whether the distance is per leg or summed over components (i)+(ii) for biochar is also unspecified. Recommend: count it if the round trip is ≥200 km per origin-destination group.
- **A14 (Negative or zero results, p.33).** There is no guidance on a negative ER_y or on biochar produced in one year but used the next year. Eq. 1 is keyed to year y "produced"; the 1-year use window allows a cross-year lag. The platform must choose whether to attribute by production year or application year. The methodology credits only biochar that has been used.
- **A15 (Uncertainty / VCS v5.0).** VM0044 v1.2 (June 2025) has no uncertainty section. VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0 (Dec 2025) §2.4 requires methodologies to address uncertainty. Check whether Verra has issued a v1.3 or a revision notice before implementing.
- **A16 (GWP source, p.35).** The value 28 (AR5) is consistent with VCS Standard v5.0 Table 9. Make it configurable ("latest VCS Standard"). In the v1.2 conversion, "28" sits beside the footnote marker 27 (AR5). It is the value, not a footnote reference (confirmed: v1.1 p.35 shows "Value applied 28").
- **A17 (F_Cp symbol, p.22).** The repeated p in F_Cp,t,p ("Cp" is part of the symbol name, then facility p) is notational only.
- **A18 (Moisture basis, pp.37-38).** For dry mass, the methodology asks for the weighted average moisture per monitoring period. If moisture is captured per batch, dry mass can be computed per batch. Both are consistent; per batch is more accurate.
- **A19 (Facility tech status, p.21).** A facility that fails any high tech condition "must be categorized immediately" as low tech. The platform needs time-stamped tech status per facility, which may change within a year. Non-soil uses then become ineligible for that biochar.
