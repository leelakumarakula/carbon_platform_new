# 1 SUMMARY DESCRIPTION 

Projects applying VCS methodology VM0047 Afforestation, Reforestation, and Revegetation 

must use this module to estimate leakage. Projects applying VM0042 Improved Agricultural Land Management or VM0032 Methodology for the Adoption of Sustainable Grasslands through Adjustment of Fire and Grazing may use this module where permitted by the respective methodology. 

This module accounts for leakage related to the displacement of pre-project agricultural activities (including grazing) and fuelwood collection activities. It includes activity-shifting leakage caused by the baseline agent and market leakage caused by other actors. 

The module also accounts for leakage mitigation. Leakage mitigation reduces the amount of prior production in the project area that is subject to leakage. Leakage mitigation may occur outside the project area where the project proponent supports interventions to increase productivity through agricultural intensification, or through cross-commodity leakage mitigation where new commodities are produced in the project area. 

The module estimates net leakage based on three factors: 

• Leakage: Displacement of agricultural activities inside the project area to locations outside of the project area as a result of project activities. This module calculates the proportion of displaced production that results in clearing of previously uncultivated lands and subsequent emission of greenhouse gases (GHGs). 

• Cross-commodity production: Introduction of new agricultural commodities to the project area, which can reduce the amount of displaced production. Any incremental increase in GHG emissions resulting from cross-commodity production is accounted for as project emissions. 

• Leakage mitigation: Increased production of pre-project commodities outside of the project area through intensification. This reduces leakage but requires accounting of the incremental increase in GHG emissions from leakage mitigation activities.

# 3 DEFINITIONS 

In addition to the definitions set out in the most recent version of the VCS Program Definitions ,the following definitions apply to this module. 

Agricultural activity 

Activity resulting in the production of an agricultural commodity on agricultural land 

Agricultural commodity 

Any (part of) plant, animal, or animal product, produced on agricultural land 

Cross-commodity production 

Production of an agricultural commodity that is introduced into the project area as part of the project activity. Differs from pre-project commodity production and is accounted for as a change in monitored production. 

Fuelwood 

Wood used as fuel, including firewood or charcoal produced from woody biomass 

Land sparing 

Net decrease in land area required for agricultural production due to an increase in the production of a commodity introduced into the project area; assumed to have occurred when the value for net new land brought into production is negative 

Leakage mitigation 

Observed increase in commodity production above a historic baseline, that results from clearly identifiable leakage mitigation activities occurring outside of the project area (e.g., agricultural intensification) 

Leakage mitigation activity start date 

Date on which an activity that increases commodity production for the purpose of leakage mitigation is implemented outside of the project area 

Subsistence commodity 

Agricultural commodity that is not destined for commercial markets VMD0054, v1.1

Project proponents must meet all applicability conditions of the version of VM0032, VM0042,

# 5 PROCEDURES 

Leakage emissions from project activities are calculated for the entire project area and for at least five years from the project start date. 1 For grouped projects, leakage must be calculated for all instances for five years after the most recent addition of an instance. 

## 5.1 Step 1: Determine Change in Production in Project Area 

Baseline agricultural activities and fuelwood commodity production in the project area must be estimated using historical production records for each commodity during the historical reference period. Use the process in Sections  5.1.1 –5.1.3  to estimate historical production for each commodity. 

5.1.1 Step 1.1: Determine the Historical Reference Period 

The historical reference period must be the longer of the following: 

1)  The three-year period immediately preceding the project or instance start date 

2)  One complete crop rotation

Leakage from displacement is calculated over a period of at least five years to capture the incremental changes in foregone production resulting from assumed increases in yield. The incremental increase does not result in repeated or annual land conversion. ## 5.2 Step 2: Determine Impact of Leakage Mitigation Activities (Optional) 

Project proponents may implement activities outside the project area that reduce the amount of leakage. The areas in which such activities take place are called leakage mitigation areas. Where leakage mitigation activities are implemented, the project proponent must: 

1)  ensure the leakage mitigation area is located outside of the project area. 

2)  ensure that the leakage mitigation area is in the same country as the project. VMD0054, v1.1 

9

3)  secure exclusive claim to a specific amount of increased commodity production within a leakage mitigation area through an agreement with the landowner or manager responsible for implementing leakage mitigation activities. The agreement must specify the amount of increased production attributable to the leakage mitigation activities, as a percentage or absolute value. 

4)  account for leakage mitigation only in the years during which an agreement is in place. The terms of the agreement must be no less than five years. 

Note – Leakage mitigation may occur on land owned or managed by individuals or groups participating in the project, but land used for leakage mitigation must not be included in the project area. 

5)  account for significant increases in GHG emissions associated with leakage mitigation activities. All projects applying leakage mitigation activities must use Appendix 2 of 

VM0047 to identify significant emission sources and report any net increase attributable to leakage mitigation activities (e.g., fertilizers, fossil fuel consumption). Where supplemental feeding is used, account for emissions from increased production of supplemental feedstocks. 

6)  not exceed maximum carrying capacity nor deplete vegetation and soil resources where leakage mitigation involves livestock intensification. Project proponents must demonstrate the maximum carrying capacity with reports prepared by, or signed attestations from, qualified independent local experts (e.g., agricultural extension agent, professional agronomist). 

7)  where such activities include fuelwood production, ensure that this only involves the establishment of new tree plantations. 

The impact of leakage mitigation activities is determined using the steps outlined in Sections 

5.2.1 –5.2.2 .

## 5.4 Step 4: Determine Change in Carbon Stocks in New Lands Brought into Production VMD0054, v1.1

This module assumes that new land brought into production is forest land unless the project proponent demonstrates one of the following: 

1)  Forest land is extremely rare, constituting less than 10% of land cover in the country in which the project is located. 

2)  Forest land is effectively protected or otherwise highly unlikely to be converted (e.g., forests are located on steep slopes or only at high elevations). To demonstrate that forests are unlikely to be converted, the annual gross deforestation rate of the country in which the project is located must be less than 0.1% over the past ten years. Global Forest Watch 4 must be used to substantiate the gross deforestation rate. 

Where either of the above conditions are met, the project proponent may proceed as follows: 

a)  Identify the relevant ecoregion where the project is located. 5

b)  Assume that this ecoregion land cover type is converted as the portion of new land brought into production ( AL t). 

c)  Estimate the change in biomass carbon stock ( ΔCbiomass ) of all significant carbon pools and the change in soil organic carbon stock ( ΔSOC) caused by this conversion. 

Where conditions (1) –(2) are not met, the project proponent must assume the new land brought into production is forest land. The change in biomass carbon stocks in the area generating leakage emissions assumes the complete loss of above- and belowground biomass, dead wood, and litter.

# APPENDIX 1: BACKGROUND ON DEFAULT VALUES 

Growth Rate of Production 

The default value for growth rates of production was developed based on reports from the International Food Policy Research Institute (IFPRI) and Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). This default value based on the growth rates of production is applied within this module to serve as a proxy for growth rates in yields. Analysis from IFPRI indicates that global average agricultural growth rates over the past 40 years have remained less than 2.5% per decade, with values ranging from 2.08% to 2.42% (Fuglie and Nin-Pratt 2012). Reports from FAO confirm that this is a conservative default value for growth rates. FAO predicts that agricultural growth rates in developing countries will decrease in the coming decades with an average value of 1.6% from 2007 to 2030 and 0.9% from 2030 to 2050 (Alexandratos and Bruinsma 2012). Therefore, 2.5% was selected as a conservative default value for commodity production. Project proponents may justify using regional or national values instead where such data are relevant and available. 

Proportion of Leakage Resulting in Increased Supply 

The default value for the proportion of leakage resulting in increased supply of agricultural commodities outside the project area was developed considering elasticities of supply and demand as well as observed prices and quantities to develop constant elasticity of substitution (CES) demand, supply, and reduced supply curves. The analysis for developing the conservative default value used elasticities of supply and demand across commodities and countries from peer-reviewed economic studies. Data on exogenous prices and quantities of commodities in specific geographic regions were gathered from several sources including the USDA Economic Research Service (ERS), Food and Agricultural Organization of the United Nations (FAO), Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED), and Trading Economics. Elasticity data on agricultural commodities and forest products indicate that no commodities experienced perfectly inelastic supply or demand. Averaging across global commodities, production losses occurring within the project would result in a 13 –72% increase in supply elsewhere. Therefore, 75% was selected as a conservative default value. 

Proportion of Increased Supply Coming from New Land Brought into Production 

The default value for the increased supply coming from new land brought into production was developed based on data and reports from the FAO. Such studies indicate that globally only 10% of increases in agricultural production will come from new land being brought into production (Alexandratos and Bruinsma 2012). Some sub-regions such as Latin America and the Caribbean may experience 40% of future supply coming from new land being brought into production. While these studies provide predictions regarding long-term trends in production, other studies on short-term trends result in other conclusions. Roberts and Schlenker (2013) provide evidence that shorter-term price VMD0054, v1.1 

31 

shocks do not follow these longer-term trends and the increase in production is primarily made up by bringing new land into production. 

The default value has been developed based on the assumption that projects will have longer-term impacts on commodity markets. Based on FAO data, 40% was selected as a conservative threshold for the default value for agricultural commodities. This default value may be updated in the future where projects are shown to have shorter-term impacts on commodity markets. 

Conservative Assumptions Used 

Use of the above default values is further supported by the following conservative assumptions that have been employed elsewhere in this module: 

1)  New lands brought into production are assumed to be forested lands. 

2)  Positive leakage effects from ARR activities that provide timber to the market and reduce harvesting on existing forested lands are not included. Similarly, increases in production in leakage mitigation areas that occur after leakage emissions are calculated are not included. 

3)  Leakage calculations conservatively ignore that new lands brought into production outside the host country would reduce the amount of new land brought into production within the host country and would reduce leakage emissions. 7

4)  Leakage estimated in this module does not decrease with time. However, actual leakage effects may be expected to decrease as the proportion of carbon projects and the impact on market supply and prices from foregone production increase over time.
