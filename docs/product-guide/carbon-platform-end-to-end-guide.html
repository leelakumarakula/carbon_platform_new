About this document

The document covers three things:

  • The product. What the platform does at each stage of the carbon-credit lifecycle, and who does it.
  • The controls. The approval, audit and data-integrity rules that make the output defensible to a verifier, a registry and a buyer.
  • The test procedure. How a tester walks the full lifecycle in the application, what they should see at every step, and which actions must be refused.

The test results in Chapter 8 come from complete runs of the lifecycle on the local test environment on 4–5 October 2026, using dedicated test accounts.

Important status note All carbon calculation modules are labelled Not production ready until a methodology expert has reviewed each one against the official methodology document and the platform has recorded that sign-off. The test credits in this document were issued against a test registry organization and are not real credits. See Chapter 9.

1. Executive summary

The Carbon dMRV Platform is a digital measurement, reporting and verification (dMRV) system for nature-based carbon projects, focused on agriculture and smallholder farmers. It covers the whole lifecycle in one application:

  • registering farmers and their land;
  • measuring soil carbon and farm practices in the field and in the laboratory;
  • calculating emission reductions and removals under recognised methodologies;
  • verification by an independent body;
  • recording registry issuance;
  • selling and retiring the credits;
  • paying farmers their share of the revenue.

What was demonstrated

  • Complete lifecycle, end to end. A VM0042 soil-carbon project went through every stage:
    • farmer and farm onboarding;
    • two soil-sampling campaigns with control sites, and laboratory analysis;
    • a calculation of 24 VCU;
    • independent verification of 24 tCO2e;
    • registry issuance of 24 VCU, confirmed by a second person, and entry into the credit ledger;
    • a sale of 10 VCU to a buyer for INR 8,500, of which the buyer retired 4 VCU;
    • a revenue settlement, with a farmer payout of INR 4,200, paid and reconciled against the bank statement and visible to the farmer in their own login.
  • Every methodology option calculates. All 13 methodology options (one calculation module each) were run end to end. These include Gold Standard soil carbon (five variants), tree planting on farms, biochar, India CCTS afforestation and mangroves, rice methane and Gold Standard afforestation.
  • Separation of duties holds. Every negative test passed: the person who submits a record can never approve it. This covers methodology approval, farmer KYC, farm verification, eligibility, field records, lab results, calculations, issuance, ledger openings, buyer payments and settlements.

What remains before production use

  • Expert sign-off of each calculation module against the official methodology (the platform workflow for this exists; the reviews have not been done).
  • Gold Standard Paris-alignment status for vintages from 2026 onwards. The platform enforces this gate today.
  • A decision on splitting one farm into several strata (research is complete; the decision is pending).
  • Production infrastructure items listed in Chapter 9: malware-scanning engine, multi-factor sign-in, shared rate-limit store and live payment and registry integrations.

2. What the platform does

2.1 The problem it solves

Carbon credits from agriculture depend on proving, field by field, that a change in practice stored more carbon in the soil or cut emissions. The proof must survive three reviewers:

  • an independent verification body (VVB);
  • a registry (Verra, Gold Standard or India's CCTS);
  • the buyer.

Each needs to trace every credit back to a sample taken at a known GPS point on a known farm, analysed by a named laboratory, under a fixed version of the rules. Spreadsheets cannot keep that chain intact. The platform keeps every link as a database record that cannot be silently changed afterwards.

2.3 The evidence chain

At any point a reviewer can follow a credit back to its source. In the application this is the Lineage view, available on lab results, calculation runs, verification decisions, issuance batches, orders and payouts:

Farmer payout → settlement run → revenue record → order and payment → ledger transfer → credit batch (vintage, serial range) → registry issuance → registry submission (SHA-256 snapshot) → VVB decision → verification package → calculation report → approved calculation run → frozen inputs → laboratory result → sealed sample → field collection (GPS, photo) → sampling point → stratum → farm → farmer

2.4 Technology in one paragraph

The platform has three layers:

  • Web application: Angular, served to the browser.
  • Server API: Python/FastAPI.
  • Database: Microsoft SQL Server, whose geography engine measures farm areas, detects overlapping boundaries and validates GPS points.

Calculations use exact decimal arithmetic, and every step records which methodology equation produced it. Documents are stored with a SHA-256 checksum, and key tables are protected by database triggers so that history cannot be edited or deleted.

4. Controls that apply everywhere

Audit trail

Every action is logged with the user, time, organization and the reason they typed. Audit tables are append-only, enforced by database triggers. See Administration → Audit log.

Reasons required

Almost every state change asks for a reason of at least 3 characters, which is kept in the history.

"Still needed" checklists

Detail pages show what is missing before the next step is allowed. Errors name the business rule, e.g. REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET, and the field at fault.

Status history

Projects, farms, periods, runs and other records keep a full status history: who moved it, when and why.

Frozen, hashed snapshots

Datasets, calculation inputs, calculation reports, verification packages and registry submissions are frozen and fingerprinted with SHA-256. Any later change is detected.

Immutable approved records

An approved methodology version, an approved lab result or an issued batch is never edited. Changes create a new version that supersedes the old one, and both stay visible.

Documents

The file type is detected from the content, not the file name. Every file gets a SHA-256 checksum and a malware-scan hook, and suspicious files go to Document quarantine. KYC and bank documents are restricted.

Personal data protection

KYC ID numbers are stored only as a keyed fingerprint plus the last 4 digits (shown as ••••9012). Bank account numbers are encrypted. Labs, VVBs and buyers never see farmer names, contacts or GPS locations.

Sign-in security

Passwords have at least 12 characters with letters and digits. A temporary password must be changed at first sign-in. Accounts lock for 15 minutes after 5 failures. Sign-in is rate-limited, sessions are short-lived and refresh tokens rotate.

Organization isolation

Users only see records of their own organization. A record outside their scope returns "not found", so its existence is not revealed.

Environments

Records carry an environment (LIVE / TEST / DEMO). Calculation modules not yet signed off are blocked in production.

No silent recalculation

A recalculation after verification or issuance creates a new run and invalidates the old package; nothing already issued is changed.

5. How to read the stage chapters

Each stage chapter (S0–S17) follows the same layout:

  • Purpose: why the stage exists.
  • Who: the roles involved.
  • Steps: the actions in order, with the screen to use and the expected result.
  • Controls: what the platform checks or enforces.
  • Output: what the stage hands to the next one.
  • How to test: positive checks marked ✔, and negative tests marked ✖ must be refused. A refusal is a pass: it proves the control works.

Screen names in bold match the application menu exactly, e.g. Projects → MRV. The example values are the ones used in the recorded test runs.

Tester tips
  • Keep two or three browser windows open (normal, private and a second browser). Each holds its own sign-in, so testers can act as different people side by side.
  • Date boxes use month/day/year.
  • For field work, use the phone view (F12, then Ctrl+Shift+M in the browser).

Open decision: strata inside a farm Today a stratum is built from whole farms (each farm belongs to one stratum). VM0042 allows, and the rice methodology VM0051 requires, splitting a single field into several strata when soil or management differs within it. Research with methodology quotes is complete; the proposal is to allow stratum polygons drawn inside farms while keeping whole-farm strata as the default. This is awaiting a management decision (Chapter 9).

9.2 Production infrastructure

  • Malware scanning: the scanning hook exists; a real antivirus engine must be connected (development detects only the EICAR test file).
  • Multi-factor sign-in: data model ready; enrolment flow not yet built.
  • Rate limiting: in-memory today; a shared store (Redis) is needed before running more than one server.
  • Payments and registries: buyer payments, farmer payouts and registry submissions are recorded manually with references and PDF evidence. Live bank and registry integrations are not connected.
  • Encryption-key rotation: rotating the data encryption key needs a re-encryption job (not built). The key must be kept in a secret store with a backup.
  • Known bug: re-opening data collection on a period whose dataset was already submitted can leave the period stuck (S10).

9.3 Not in scope of the current build

  • VM0047 area-based approach; rice Quantification Approaches 1 and 2.
  • Use of external satellite or soil data as calculation inputs (reference only, by design).

Appendix D: glossary

TermMeaning
dMRVDigital measurement, reporting and verification.
MethodologyThe official rulebook (e.g. Verra VM0042) that defines when a project qualifies and how its credits are calculated.
Calculation moduleThe platform's implementation of a methodology's equations, attached to a methodology version.
SOCSoil organic carbon.
StratumAn area with similar conditions (soil, climate, management) that is sampled and calculated as one unit.
Control siteComparable land outside the project, used under VM0042 to measure what would have happened without the project (the baseline).
Monitoring periodThe time window for which data is collected and credits are calculated; Period 0 is the baseline sampling.
DatasetThe frozen, QA-approved collection of all monitoring data for a period.
Uncertainty deductionCredits removed when measurement uncertainty is above the methodology's threshold.
BufferShare of credits set aside in a pooled reserve against future reversal (non-permanence risk).
VVB / ACVAValidation and Verification Body (Verra, Gold Standard) / Accredited Carbon Verification Agency (India CCTS).
VCU / GS VER / CCCCredit units: Verified Carbon Unit (Verra), Gold Standard Verified Emission Reduction, Carbon Credit Certificate (India CCTS).
VintageThe year in which the reduction or removal happened.
RetirementPermanent use of a credit to claim an offset; it can never be resold.
Separation of dutiesThe rule that the person who submits something cannot approve it.
SHA-256 hashA digital fingerprint; any change to the data changes the fingerprint, so tampering is detectable.
KYCKnow Your Customer: identity verification of farmers and buyers.
